01 · Summary

Humanization, developability, and formulation — on one platform

TheraMab brings together on one platform the three things needed to take an antibody from a non-human sequence to a developable candidate: humanization design, developability and manufacturability assessment, and CMC formulation optimization and advice. The client enters one heavy chain and one light chain and receives multiple humanization designs, the risk positions in each design, actionable optimized sequences, and formulation advice tiered for both subcutaneous and intravenous administration.

Key Points
  • One workflow, no tool switching: humanization, assessment, optimization, and formulation advice all happen in one place, and an optimized sequence can go straight back into the assessment table to be re-assessed.
  • Built around privacy and information security: each client's sequences and results belong only to their own account, and neither the pages nor the interfaces expose internal details of the algorithms or databases.
  • Results with context: each parameter is placed within a reference range derived from our clinical observations and estimates and shown with a clear position label, not as an isolated number.

Antibodies that are already fully human or humanized (including sequences designed with other software) can also go directly to developability assessment. This product is provided for scientific research and early R&D reference only and is not a basis for clinical decisions, diagnosis, treatment, or regulatory submissions.

03 · Background and Challenges

Why early antibody work loses information between tools

In early antibody R&D, sequence design and developability judgments are usually spread across several tools and several people, so information is hard to connect. Three problems come up again and again.

1
Humanization and developability are disconnected
Humanization designs are made in one tool and developability is assessed in another. Changing one sequence means exporting and re-importing again, and the trade-offs of back-mutation and their effect on developability cannot be seen on the same chart.
2
Risk is only numbers, with no sense of position
A single physicochemical parameter lacks a reference, so the team cannot tell whether a value is high or low, nor in which direction to optimize.
3
Formulation and sequence are considered separately
Formulation is only considered after the sequence is fixed; by the time viscosity or aggregation problems appear it is already late. There is no direct link between risk sites and the corresponding formulation levers.

There is also a concern for external use: sequences are a client's most core asset, and a tool that lets multiple users' data mix together, or that exposes internal algorithm and database details on the page, is hard to accept. TheraMab is designed to make the three things above a single workflow, while treating privacy and information security as a prerequisite of the product rather than an add-on.

05 · Workflow

From input sequence to formulation advice

From the input sequence to the formulation advice, the client works through one continuous flow, and the result of each step carries over to the next.

  1. Input: paste or upload the heavy and light chains (CSV, FASTA, Excel; templates and format notes are provided). A non-human antibody goes through humanization; a sequence that is already fully human or humanized can go directly to developability assessment.
  1. Humanization: the system offers several framework pairings, each with different back-mutation levels (none, conservative, balanced, aggressive). The client selects the pairings of interest, and later assessment is shown only for the selected pairings.
  1. Assessment: humanness, physicochemical developability, FlashTAP, and a CMC summary. Each parameter is shown within its reference range using a position map and a radar chart, with axis notes and result analysis.
  2. Optimization: risk sites are split into a CDR table and an FR table. FR sites come with substitution suggestions; check them to generate an optimized sequence in one click, which can be exported or added back to the assessment table.
  3. Re-assessment: once an optimized sequence is added back to the assessment table it is re-assessed automatically, with results for the whole antibody, CDR-only, and FR-only, so you can see directly whether the optimization improved the key parameters (steps 4 to 5 can be repeated).
  4. Formulation advice: formulation advice tiered for subcutaneous and intravenous routes, formulation codes, and marketed molecules that are physicochemically similar; each risk site's formulation lever links to a specific formulation in the knowledge base.
  5. Library and report: candidate sequences can enter the client's own account library, and a comprehensive report is generated that includes the formulation advice and its rationale.

Analysis state is saved per job in the client's own browser, so after viewing the knowledge base or switching pages you can return to the previous results; clicking “New analysis” clears everything in one click to start over, without affecting sequences already in the library.

06 · Core Capabilities

Humanization, assessment, optimization, and formulation

Humanization

For a non-human antibody, two paths are offered in parallel: classic CDR grafting, and a neural-network (NN) design that keeps more of the parent framework. Both paths have four back-mutation levels (none, conservative, balanced, aggressive) and use the same back-mutation rules. The difference is the starting point: CDR grafting grafts onto an entire germline framework and has more back-mutation positions to consider, while NN keeps most of the parent framework and has fewer positions to consider. At step 3 the client selects the framework pairings of interest, and later assessment is shown only for the selected pairings.

Developability and manufacturability assessment

  • Physicochemical and structural parameters: instability index, hydrophobicity (GRAVY), chemical risk sites, pI, Fv net charge, the viscosity indicator FvCSP, hydrophobic patches (SAP), and aggregation propensity (A3D).
  • FlashTAP: a TAP-like rapid risk indicator.
  • Position map and radar chart: each parameter is placed within a reference range derived from our clinical observations and estimates, shown with “pass, caution, exceeds, falls short” position labels, with axis notes and result analysis. These are position labels, not a pass/fail judgment from wet-lab work, and not a release criterion.
  • Experimental value entry table: the client enters their own measured values (Tm, expression level, HIC, SMAC, AC-SINS, PSR, accelerated-stability slope, BVP, and so on); the system marks the position against thresholds and reminds the user to run a head-to-head comparison against clinical antibodies under the same conditions.
  • Knowledge base: an “Assays and developability” page explains the meaning, risk direction, relationship to developability, and limitations of each experimental and computed parameter.

Risk-site optimization

CDR and FR risk sites are presented separately. CDR sites involve binding, so only formulation and process hints are given; FR sites come with specific substitution suggestions and alternatives, and conservative handling is kept for positions such as conserved tryptophan. After the client checks the suggestions, an optimized sequence is generated in one click, which can be exported as FASTA or added back to the assessment table for re-assessment, viewed under the whole-antibody, CDR-only, and FR-only lenses to see whether it improved.

CMC formulation optimization and advice

  • Tiered advice: for the subcutaneous and intravenous administration routes, each with high, medium, and low concentration tiers, giving pH, buffer system, excipients, and viscosity risk.
  • Formulation code and comparison: a formulation code is given, along with marketed molecules that are physicochemically similar to the client's sequence for reference.
  • Levers linked to formulations: each risk site maps to a formulation lever (for example, an oxidation site maps to practices that suppress oxidation) and links to specific formulation entries in the knowledge base (a few references at most).
  • Report: a “Formulation advice and rationale” page lists composition, pH, excipients, viscosity notes, and lever rationale, marked as rule-based hints that require experimental validation.

The formulation-related outputs are suggestions based on rules and surrogate indicators; they are not a viscosity-prediction model and not experimentally validated conclusions.

07 · Privacy and Information Security

Privacy and security as a prerequisite

Privacy and information security are a prerequisite of the product. The table below lists the measures already implemented in the web edition; each has been independently reviewed and validated.

AspectMeasure
Data isolationEach client's sequence library and jobs are stored under their own account and cannot be seen by other accounts; accessing another user's job and accessing a non-existent job return exactly the same result; trial example sequences are shared read-only.
Access controlLogin authentication, with accounts opened by our invitation; account identity is passed in and verified by the access layer, and identity headers forged by the client are discarded; without a valid identity, all write operations are rejected.
Algorithm and database confidentialityPages and interfaces do not show source names, database names, sample sizes, or literature citations; external outputs drop internal fields and use neutral codes for internal references; page scripts are minified with comments removed; public interfaces are rate-limited.
Network securitySite-wide HTTPS, with HSTS, security headers, hidden server version, rate limiting, and request-body size limits; the old direct-connect ports are closed to the outside.
Client-side stateAnalysis state and drafts are kept only in the client's own browser and are not uploaded to the server; “New analysis” clears them in one click.
Backup and recoveryDaily consistency backups of the sequence library and account information, weekly recovery tests, and health checks and alerts.
Audit and change controlEvery release is first independently reviewed, then accepted in an isolated environment and independently verified by another person, with a rollback method kept ready at all times.

Data note: the platform stores the sequences, computed results, and library entries that the client submits. Only the client's own account can see them; operations staff access them only for operations and for deletion at the client's request. They are kept until the client requests deletion, with at most about two more months retained in backups. For deletion requests, contact the TheraMab team member who opened your account.

Boundaries, stated plainly: we currently use account-level data isolation and access control, without third-party security certification or an independent penetration test; backups are currently on the same host, and an off-site copy is not yet in place; operations staff can read private data files at the server level. For highly sensitive sequences, we recommend the locally run desktop edition.

09 · Scope and Limitations

What clients should know in advance

This is a reference tool for scientific research and early R&D; the following boundaries should be known to the client in advance.

  • A position label is not an experimental result: “pass, caution, exceeds, falls short” indicate where a candidate sits within the reference range, not the success or failure of wet-lab work, and not a release condition. Thresholds change under different experimental conditions; we recommend a same-condition comparison against a clinical antibody.
  • Formulation is rule-based advice: it is based on rules and surrogate indicators, not a viscosity-prediction model, and needs experimental validation.
  • Humanness comparison for external sequences has gaps: for a sequence that is already fully human or designed by other software, there is no OASis humanness score; when no parent sequence is provided, comparison to the parent (loops, H3, Vernier, interface, back-mutation) is shown as not assessed.
  • Unsupported molecule formats: single-chain antibodies (scFv) and nanobodies (VHH) are not currently supported.
  • Scale limits: up to 12 pairs assessed at a time; the assessment interface allows at most 15 calls per 5 minutes.
  • Security boundaries: see “Boundaries, stated plainly” in the previous section.
This product is for scientific research and early R&D reference and is not a basis for clinical decisions, diagnosis, treatment, or regulatory submissions. Before any candidate enters development, it needs an independent judgment by qualified personnel together with experiments and regulatory requirements.
10 · Roadmap and Disclaimer

What is coming next

Coming soon (with the next release): on page 1, an “Already a fully human antibody — go straight to developability analysis” option and a “New analysis” button; format notes and a CSV template for batch upload, with automatic assessment after import; formulation codes, similar marketed molecules, and substitution suggestions for optimized sequences; restoring analysis state from any return path; and a data and privacy page.

Planned, scheduled as demand dictates:

  • Connecting the OASis humanness score into the assessment of external sequences.
