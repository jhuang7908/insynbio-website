Background & Rationale
Why Re-design CDRs After Affinity Maturation?

The SR-R2 G49A+F112L champion achieves sub-nanomolar binding; however, its CDR similarity to the original murine scaffold remains high — a potential liability for freedom-to-operate (FTO). De novo CDR redesign using structure-constrained generative models diversifies sequence space while preserving the binding scaffold. This work establishes a re-maturation workflow that recovers affinity without reverting to original sequences.

Strategic Objectives

  • › Expand CDR sequence space for FTO and patent differentiation
  • › Maintain HER2 domain IV epitope overlap (structural constraint)
  • › Recover binding affinity via physics-guided re-maturation
  • › Reduce CDR-associated immunogenicity risk

Clinical Relevance

CDR redesign is critical for camelid-derived VHHs where original sequences may trigger ADA responses. Redesigning CDRs with human germline-like patterns while maintaining affinity is an emerging strategy for "CDR-humanized" nanobodies with improved clinical safety profiles.

The Affinity Valley: Interface Contacts Collapsed from 80 to 68

Generative models optimize for sequence naturalness, not binding energy. Upon redesign, contact count dropped 15%, salt bridges fell from 92 to 40, and Kd degraded 92-fold to 49.75 nM. Re-maturation is mandatory.

Phase III — Physics-Guided Re-maturation
Recovering the Interface Through Targeted Polar Mutations

We applied a polar-priority algorithm to WT-0838. Standard mutations required ΔΔG < −0.5 kcal/mol, while polar substitutions (Arg, Tyr, His) used a relaxed threshold with mandatory PRODIGY re-validation.

Key Insight: Salt Bridge Recovery

L64K: Directly restores electrostatic interaction with HER2, recovering +15.2 kcal/mol in MM/GBSA. W55R: Initially rejected by standard gates, but Arg guanidinium enables bifurcated H-bonding, validated by PRODIGY recheck.
