Neoantigen Scanning

Personalized Neoantigen Identification and Prioritization

Identify and rank tumor-specific neoantigens from somatic mutations. We combine MHC-I and MHC-II binding prediction with expression filtering, clonal fraction scoring, and IEDB cross-validation to deliver a shortlist of high-confidence neoantigen candidates suitable for personalized vaccine design.

  • MHC-I binding prediction — MHCflurry 2.0 + NetMHCpan 4.1; IC50 and %rank for patient HLA type; 8–11 mer peptides
  • MHC-II binding prediction — NetMHCIIpan 4.0; 15-mer peptides for CD4⁺ T-cell help; HLA-DR allele coverage
  • Neoantigen prioritization — Binding rank + tumor expression (TPM) + variant allele fraction (VAF); clonal neoantigen preference
  • IEDB cross-validation — Flag epitopes with known cross-reactivity to self or pathogen antigens; reduce autoimmunity risk
  • Population coverage analysis — Report HLA A/B/C + DR coverage across major ethnic groups for selected epitopes

Input: VCF or mutation list + patient HLA type (4-digit). Tumor expression data (RNA-seq FPKM/TPM) optional but recommended.

Heteroclitic TAA Design

Anchor-Modified Peptide Engineering for Shared Tumor-Associated Antigens

Wild-type TAA peptides often bind MHC with low affinity, resulting in weak T-cell activation. Heteroclitic design substitutes anchor residues (P2, P9 for MHC-I; P1, P9 for MHC-II) to improve MHC binding while preserving or enhancing TCR recognition of the unmodified tumor antigen — a strategy validated in clinical vaccine trials (e.g., MART-1/Melan-A ELA→ILA, gp100 g209-2M).

  • Anchor residue mapping — MHC contact analysis for patient HLA type; identify P2/P9 anchor positions
  • Substitution library generation — Generate single and double anchor variants; filter by predicted IC50 (<50 nM target)
  • Cross-reactivity scoring — Predict TCR activation cross-reactivity against wild-type TAA peptide; prioritize high cross-reactivity candidates
  • Clinical precedent review — Cross-reference with IEDB and NCI TAA database for published heteroclitic variants
  • Immunogenicity assessment — MHC-II T-cell epitope risk in modified peptide; flag de novo immunogenicity introduced by substitution

Input: Wild-type TAA peptide sequence or antigen gene name + HLA type. Target TAA may be selected from our knowledge base.

Multi-Epitope mRNA Vaccine Design

Synthesis-Ready mRNA Construct from Selected Epitopes

Assemble a multi-epitope mRNA vaccine construct from a prioritized epitope shortlist. We design the linker architecture, optimize codons for human expression (RSCU-based), and annotate the full construct for mRNA-LNP or viral vector delivery — delivering a synthesis-ready FASTA, GenBank file, and rationale report.

  • Epitope ordering and orientation — Systematic evaluation of epitope arrangement to minimize junctional epitope formation; MHC binding at junctions flagged
  • Linker design — MHC-I: AAY (proteasomal cleavage enhancer); MHC-II: GPGPG (flexible, cleavage-permissive); N-terminal adjuvant fusion peptide optional (PADRE, flagellin fragment)
  • Codon optimization — Human RSCU codon adaptation; CAI target ≥0.80; GC content 50–60%; avoid rare codons, CpG motifs (optional), and restriction sites (BsaI, BsmBI, NotI)
  • 5′/3′ UTR design — Human β-globin 5′ UTR (or synthetic); AES/mtRNR1 3′ UTR scaffold; poly-A tail recommendation
  • Delivery platform recommendation — mRNA-LNP vs. viral vector (Ad5, MVA) selection with rationale based on target, indication, and cold-chain constraints

Input: Finalized epitope list (from neoantigen scanning or heteroclitic design) + target delivery platform preference.

pMHC-TCR Peptide Design

Epitope Optimization for TCR-T Cell Therapy and pMHC Tetramer Design

Design peptides that optimally activate defined TCR clones — relevant for TCR-T cell therapy (TCR gene transfer), personalized pMHC tetramers, and vaccine-to-adoptive-cell-transfer bridging strategies. Our clinical TCR database (PDB-verified, Phase I+ or FDA-approved) provides the structural basis for peptide contact analysis and affinity-guided modifications.

  • TCR-pMHC contact mapping — CDR3α/CDR3β contact residue analysis from PDB structures; identify peptide positions critical for TCR recognition
  • Peptide variant generation — Single substitutions at TCR-contact positions; rank variants by predicted TCR binding compatibility while maintaining MHC-I binding (%rank ≤2%)
  • Clinical TCR matching — Cross-reference target epitope against our 5-clone PDB-verified clinical TCR database; flag existing validated TCRs for the same epitope
  • pMHC tetramer design — Biotinylation site guidance, MHC allomorph selection, and refolding condition recommendations for tetramer production

Input: Target peptide/antigen + HLA allele + desired TCR clone (or matched from database). PDB file of TCR-pMHC complex preferred; modeled if unavailable.
