Our research reveals that immunogenicity risk in fully human (FH) and humanized (HU) antibodies is driven by fundamentally different mechanisms. We therefore established the ADA V2 Dual-Track Prediction Model, using separate evaluation parameters and weighting systems for each category.

FH
Fully Human Antibodies · FH
Structural defects are the primary immunogenicity driver

Fully human antibodies are obtained via phage display or transgenic mouse platforms. Their CDRs evolve through somatic hypermutation (SHM), with fully human frameworks. ADA risk does not stem from foreign sequences, but from physical defects in the molecular structure itself: interface instability and exposed hydrophobic patches.

① Interface Stability · Weight 35%
ρ = +0.369

VH-VL contact count · p=0.007 (highly significant). Fewer interface contacts → higher dissociation risk → significantly elevated ADA.

② Surface Hydrophobicity · Weight 30%
ρ = +0.311

Surface hydrophobic patch count · p=0.024. Hydrophobic patches induce aggregation and activate APC presentation — primary FH immunogenicity mechanism.

③ VL Germline Deviation · Weight 20%
ρ = +0.264

VL germline identity (inverted) · p=0.056. Greater SHM divergence from the human repertoire → higher ADA risk.

④ MHC-II Epitopes · Weight 10%
ρ = +0.134

MHC-II strong-binding peptide count. SHM-derived epitopes are the ultimate ADA trigger, though in FH their effect is attenuated by background tolerance.

ADA V2 Dual-Track Calibration (April 2026): FH-specific score corrected two weighting errors: ① Interface contact normalization ceiling raised 60 → 100 (FH mean ≈ 71; old standard compressed nearly all FH antibodies to I = 1.0, losing discriminatory power); ② VL germline deviation changed to (1 − VL_identity) × 0.20 (old adjustment range was only ±0.004, near-zero contribution). HU
Humanized Antibodies · HU
Design-induced risk: Vernier Zone & CDR junction epitopes

Humanized antibodies graft murine CDRs onto human frameworks. ADA risk comes not only from foreign CDR peptides, but also from three engineering-specific "design traps": Vernier Zone residues, CDR-FR junction epitopes, and humanization-induced molecular instability. Clinical interventions (e.g., MTX co-administration) significantly suppress apparent ADA, making molecular-level correlation signals statistically weaker — exactly why HU assessment requires a dedicated system.

① Vernier Zone Audit · Weight 25%
Deep Scan

Vernier Zone lies below CDR loops (VH:27–30, 47–49, 67–71; VL:35–36, 46–49). Buried murine residues release hydrophobic anchor peptides upon proteolytic degradation, captured by MHC-II with high affinity.

② CDR-FR Junction Epitopes · Weight 15%
Junction

CDR (murine) / FR (human) junctions create "hybrid peptides" never found in natural sequences — these Junction Epitopes are immunogenicity triggers unique to humanized antibodies.

③ Aggregation Propensity · Weight 20%
ρ = +0.196

Aggregation motif count · p=0.110 (HU high-sensitivity subset, n=73). Improper Vernier zone handling disrupts hydrophobic core balance — the strongest physical mechanism triggering ADA.

④ Germline Identity · Weight 30%
Identity

VH/VL germline homology assessment against 842 clinical antibody reference database. Ensures framework "human similarity" compliance baseline.

⑤ Instability Correction · Weight 10%
Baseline

Thermodynamic instability coefficient and clinical background correction (e.g., immunosuppressant co-medication), as final risk expectation adjustment.

ADA V2.1 Composite Score: FH vs HU Predictive Power

The ADA V2.1 composite score integrates multi-dimensional features into a single risk indicator. In the FH subset, the composite score shows a statistically significant positive correlation with clinical ADA (ρ = +0.308, p = 0.025), further improved over V2.0.

April 2026 Data Validation

Key improvement in this weight update: based on FH deep-scan results, interface contact density (I) weight raised from 20% → 35%, surface patch (P) weight raised from 25% → 30%, and a FH-specific Midpoint correction (0.32) introduced for balanced risk stratification. In the HU subset, apparent signals are suppressed by widespread clinical immunosuppressant use (ρ ≈ 0) — exactly validating the necessity of the dual-track approach.

Prediction Methods

The InSynBio immunogenicity assessment integrates complementary multi-layer analyses, with the final report automatically switching between FH/HU-specific weighting systems based on antibody type:

  • IEDB MHC-II Deep Scan — Covers 27 HLA-DR alleles; HU-specific additionally scans Vernier Zone (~10 positions per VH/VL) and CDR-FR junctions (5 residues at each CDR end) for degradation peptides.

  • Risk Site Clustering — Identifies spatially clustered T-cell epitope hotspots, distinguishing isolated weak signals from functional strong epitope clusters.

  • Surface Exposure Assessment — Parker hydrophilicity scoring + SASA analysis (when PDB provided); FH-specific focuses on surface hydrophobic patch count.

  • Germline Tolerance Filtering — IGHV/IGKV/IGLV frequency-weighted filter identifying non-self human residues; HU-specific additionally evaluates Vernier Zone residue humanization degree.

  • ADA V2 Dual-Track Composite Score — Automatically selects the FH or HU weight matrix based on input sequence type, outputs stratified risk scores (Low / Moderate / High), benchmarked against 138 clinical records.

All scores are benchmarked against 138 clinical records in the InSynBio ADA database, providing percentile ranking and reference comparisons (e.g., Herceptin®, Humira® benchmarks).

April 2026 Data Validation — Latest high-sensitivity assay stratification analysis (n=122, ECL/Tiered) confirms: FH system's interface stability signal (ρ=+0.339, p=0.018) and MHC-II epitope signal (ρ=+0.425, p=0.034) remain statistically significant after removing assay bias, confirming ADA V2 dual-track robustness. 8/8 previously significant features maintain consistent direction across the full sample.
