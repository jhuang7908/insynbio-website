Antibody CMC Developability
Three-Format Panoramic Benchmark

Based on 934 clinical-stage therapeutic antibody sequences (VH/VL×840, VHH×42, scFv×52), systematically scored across four CMC dimensions using InSynBio AbEvaluator to establish industry-level developability reference ranges. Whether your molecule is a conventional IgG, nanobody, or scFv scaffold, use this benchmark for a pre-development checkup — data-driven identification of high-risk candidates before entering costly CMC development workflows.

🩺
Like a Population Health Checkup — for Your Antibody

Just as a medical checkup compares your results against healthy population reference ranges, this antibody checkup compares your candidate against the CMC parameter distributions of antibodies that have already entered clinical trials. Different formats (IgG / VHH / scFv) each have their own "normal value ranges" — a pI value normal in an IgG context may be elevated in a VHH context. This benchmark identifies high-risk candidates before CMC development begins, avoiding hundreds of thousands to millions in wasted development costs.

ADI Composite Developability Index

ADI (Antibody Developability Index) is a weighted composite score from 0 to 100. The scoring algorithm is based on InSynBio's proprietary multi-dimensional weighting model, integrating four CMC dimensions. Weighting coefficients are calibrated by clinical data regression (specific weights are proprietary and not disclosed):

DimensionParametersScoring Logic
🟦 HydrophobicityGRAVY, hydrophobic patch (max 9-mer), SAP aggregation propensity scorePiecewise linear scoring by reference population percentile
🟩 Charge DistributionpI, net charge at pH 7, charge patch (max 7-mer)Symmetric tent function; full score at p50
🟨 Chemical Modification RiskN-glycosylation, deamidation (NG/NS), isomerization (DG/DS), oxidation, free CysFewer sites = better score
🟥 Aggregation RiskAggregation motif count, hydrophobic cluster count, instability indexMulti-dimensional composite gating

VHH Aggregation Score Lower (48.0): Clinical VHH sequences commonly carry more aggregation motifs (mean 3.7 vs. 0–2 for VH/VL), driven by CDR3 length and its tendency to form atypical secondary structures. However, VHH's excellent chemical modification score (70.4) compensates — this is the molecular basis for nanobodies' superior thermal stability.

scFv Hydrophobicity and Chemical Modification Lower: scFv concatenates VH and VL into a single chain, exposing a larger hydrophobic surface (no VH-VL interface shielding), while dual CDR sets double the total count of chemical modification sites (deamidation, isomerization). The (G₄S)₃ linker is itself highly hydrophilic and introduces no additional risk.
