ACTES Intelligent Decision Engine

The core logic of the ACTES Decision Engine: antigen features drive architecture selection — not the reverse. The engine uses clinical co-occurrence data from 237 components with antigen attribute analysis to generate personalized construct recommendations:

  • ① Antigen Profiling: Integrates antigen density, epitope location (membrane-distal vs. membrane-proximal), heterogeneity, and normal tissue expression to generate target feasibility scores.
  • ② Cell Therapy Modality Selection: Auto-routes to CAR-T (adaptive immunity), CAR-NK (innate + allogeneic), or CAR-M (stroma remodeling) based on disease type and microenvironment characteristics.
  • ③ Signaling Domain-Density Matching: Low-density antigens → CD28 co-stimulation (rapid activation); high-density antigens → 4-1BB (persistence); ultra-low density → tandem dual signaling.
  • ④ Hinge-Epitope Spatial Matching: AlphaFold2-predicted epitope-to-membrane distance auto-selects short/medium/long hinge to avoid immunological synapse geometric mismatch.
  • ⑤ Affinity Engineering: High normal-tissue targets (HER2, EGFR) → affinity attenuation to protect normal tissue; low-density targets (GPC3) → affinity enhancement for recognition efficiency.
  • ⑥ Solid Tumor Barrier Response: Solid tumor indication auto-triggers armoring modules (TGFB_DNR, IL-7/CCL19 secretion), stroma co-targeting (CAR-M), and chemokine receptor expression.
  • ⑦ Safety Switch Selection: iCasp9 (rapid elimination, >95% clearance), EGFRt (ADCC-mediated elimination), or logic-gated SynNotch AND-gate matched to target safety window.
  • ⑧ Delivery Vector & Manufacturing Path: Lentivirus, non-viral transposon, or in vivo LNP delivery selected based on CAR gene size and clinical scenario.

Strategic Design Tracks

We design systems, not just constructs. Our platform covers the four industry and scientific frontiers that are defining the next generation of cell therapy.

Industry Frontiers

HOT

In Vivo CAR

No manufacturing, no leukapheresis. LNP-mRNA delivers CAR directly to T cells or macrophages in the patient.

Our Stack:
CD5-LNP (T-cell targeted) · ManC-LNP (macrophage) · mRNA-optimized promoters (JeT compact) · Transient expression = built-in safety
Barriers: Biodistribution, off-target transduction, repeat dosing
HOT

Allogeneic (Off-the-Shelf)

Universal donor CAR-T/NK: pre-manufactured, cryobanked, immediate availability. Eliminates patient-specific manufacturing.

Our Stack:
TRAC KO + B2M KO (CRISPR) · CD47 Stealth · HLA-E/G NK evasion · PiggyBac/SB100X non-viral integration · iPSC-CAR pipelines
Barriers: GvHD, host rejection, limited persistence
HOT

Autoimmune CAR

Deep B-cell depletion for immune reset (SLE, MS, SSc), or CAR-Treg for antigen-specific tolerance induction.

Our Stack:
Anti-CD19 (FMC63) for B-cell reset · CAR-Treg (FoxP3/Helios OE, ICOS costim) · Short-lived mRNA CAR (single-shot depletion) · 4-1BB dominant (lower CRS)
Barriers: Durability of remission, B-cell aplasia management, Treg stability

Scientific Frontier

FRONTIER

Solid Tumor CAR

Case Study: GPC3 CAR-T for HCC

The ACTES engine doesn't just pick parts — it reasons through the biology. Here is the full AI deduction trace for a Hepatocellular Carcinoma project targeting GPC3.

Input   Target: GPC3  Indication: Solid Tumor (HCC)  Delivery: Autologous
D1  Disease Biology
What type of malignancy? TME characteristics?
Solid tumor — immunosuppressive (TGF-β, hypoxia), fibrotic ECM
Solid tumor track engaged. Requires armoring, homing, and safety layers beyond core CAR.
D2  Antigen Topology + Live Data
Where is the GPC3 epitope? What does UniProt tell us?
Membrane-proximal (5–10 nm) · Medium-high density on HCC · UniProt P51654: 5 glycosylation sites, 7 disulfide bonds
CD28 Medium Hinge + CD28 TM — GPC3 epitope at ~5–10 nm requires medium spacer. ✘ IgG4 Long Hinge rejected: excessive inter-membrane distance for this epitope. ✘ CD8α Short rejected: insufficient reach. UniProt data shows: 5 glycosylation sites — validate binder on glycosylated antigen.
D3  Delivery & D4 Chassis
Ex vivo or in vivo? Which effector cell? Which vector?
Autologous ex vivo → αβ T cell (standard for solid tumors). ✘ NK rejected: lower persistence. ✘ In-vivo LNP rejected: ex-vivo manufacturing is regulatory-proven.
Lentiviral (LV) — FDA-proven in 6 products, 8 kb sufficient. ✘ RV: valid but less regulatory precedent. ✘ SB: cheaper but less validated for fast-track. Engine selects from 6 vector types (LV, RV, SB, PB, AAV, mRNA-LNP).
D5  Core CAR Assembly
Signal peptide? Costimulatory domain? Activation? Hinge-TM pairing quality?
Solid tumor = exhaustion risk → persistence-biased costimulation. Hinge-TM pairing check: CD28+CD28 = acceptable (moderate tonic signaling).
CD8α SP (highest surface expression in approved CARs) · 4-1BB costim (TRAF1/2 → NF-κB → mitochondrial FAO → Tcm memory). ✘ CD28 costim rejected: PI3K/Akt/mTOR → glycolytic Teff → rapid exhaustion in solid TME; higher CRS risk. CD3ζ 3×ITAM standard activation.
D6  Armoring
What are the dominant suppression axes in HCC?
High TGF-β + Hypoxia + Dense ECM
TGFB_DNR (co-expressed via P2A) — Converts inhibitory TGF-β into survival advantage. Also considered: GPX4 (anti-ferroptosis) if lipid peroxidation is high.
D7  Safety
Is GPC3 expressed on normal tissues?
Low expression on normal liver & lung → on-target/off-tumor risk
iCasp9 Safety Switch — AP1903-inducible elimination (>95% clearance). Also equip tEGFR depletion tag for cetuximab-mediated ADCC backup.
D8  Logic Gate
Need conditional specificity?
Off-tumor risk moderate but manageable → AND-gate optional
NOT required for GPC3 (low normal-tissue expression). For HER2-type targets, engine would enforce SynNotch AND-gate or affinity-tuned Kd.
D9  Engineering & Assembly
Additional modifications? Promoter? Final assembly?
Solid tumor → benefit from anti-exhaustion + homing
c-Jun OE (anti-exhaustion TF) if exhaustion risk is critical. EF1α promoter (clinical standard), WPRE. Final: EF1α → CD8α SP-scFv-CD28 Hinge-CD28 TM-4-1BB-CD3ζ-P2A-TGFB_DNR-T2A-iCasp9 → WPRE-polyA. Codon-optimized DNA (CAI 0.76, GC 52%) + circular plasmid SVG generated.
D10  Clinical Evidence + Live Data
What does the latest literature say about GPC3 CAR-T?
Knowledge Engine retrieved 5 recent PubMed articles on GPC3 CAR-T clinical trials. No ADA data for GPC3 binders in Three-Tier database → novel target.
Clinical evidence supports GPC3 CAR-T feasibility in HCC. Latest literature highlights: persistence challenges in liver TME and combination strategies with checkpoint blockade. ADA monitoring recommended as novel-target standard.

Safety & Efficacy

Safety is engineered into the sequence — not just monitored in the clinic.

  • Safety Switches — iCasp9 (standard, >95% elimination), tEGFR (ADCC-mediated clearance), RQR8 (CD20 mimotope).
  • Logic Gating — SynNotch (AND-gate for enhanced specificity), iCAR (inhibitory CAR to protect normal tissue).
  • Toxicity Management — 4-1BB co-stimulation reduces CRS peak; locoregional delivery minimizes systemic toxicity.

Technical Methodology

ACTES (Antigen-Characteristic-based T-cell Engineering System) is powered by a database of 237 clinical CAR-T components. Combining machine learning and structural bioinformatics, it aligns antigen features, disease context, and engineering constraints to systematically identify the optimal architecture for each project.

ACTES Design Workflow (5-Step Process)

1
Antigen Feature Analysis Expression profile, epitope accessibility, tumor vs. normal tissue differential expression; on-target/off-tumor risk assessment to determine logic gating necessity
2
Binding Domain Matching Screen binding domain formats (scFv / VHH / DARPin) from clinical database with high match to target and disease type; assess affinity range and dissociation rate requirements
3
Architecture Combination Design Hinge-transmembrane-co-stimulatory domain combination optimization; strategy differentiation by disease type (hematologic / solid tumor / autoimmune); CRS/GvHD risk simulation
4
Safety & Persistence Engineering Safety switch integration (iCasp9 / tEGFR / RQR8); exhaustion risk assessment; armoring element selection (IL-15/21, PD-1 DN, TGFβ barrier)
5
Validation Plan Design Recommend in vitro (killing curves, cytokine profiling) and in vivo (PDX/syngeneic models) validation checkpoints; seamless integration with partner CROs

Common Questions

How do I start a CAR-T design project?

Complete the inquiry form below, provide your target antigen and disease indication, and we will complete a free preliminary assessment within 1–2 business days. Formal project kickoff follows scope confirmation.

Can I optimize an existing binding domain sequence?

Yes. Provide your existing scFv or VHH sequence and we will perform humanization, affinity optimization, and match it to the best-fit CAR architecture — no need to design a new binding domain from scratch.

What are the timeline and pricing?

Computational design reports are typically delivered within 5–10 business days. Pricing is project-specific based on complexity (number of targets, structural modeling scope, CRO integration). Initial consultation is free; a quote is issued after scope confirmation.

What cell chassis and delivery modalities are supported?

We support CAR-T (αβ), CAR-NK, CAR-M, γδ T cells, and emerging in vivo CAR (LNP-mRNA delivery). We recommend the most suitable chassis based on clinical stage and indication.

How is sequence and project information kept confidential?

An NDA is signed before project initiation. Your targets, sequences, and all project information are encrypted in transit and not shared with third parties. Raw data is not retained in our systems after project completion.
