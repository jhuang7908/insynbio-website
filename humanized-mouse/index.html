Next-Generation In Vivo System for Antibody Discovery and Evaluation

This project seeks joint-development partners among biopharmaceutical companies to build a next-generation in vivo system for antibody discovery and evaluation.

The tables below are organized in three directions: (1) limitations of current human antibody transgenic in vivo model platforms; (2) limitations of current transgenic humanized mice; and (3) limitations of current human immune system (HIS) mice.

1. Limitations of current human antibody transgenic in vivo model platforms

Aim: obtain higher-quality candidates that are truly developable as drugs.

Functional issue Actual limitations of existing platforms
Nanobody / single-domain antibody quality Candidates obtained on some platforms show uneven affinity, epitope coverage, stability, and nonspecificity, and still require extensive engineering optimization afterward.
Weak antigens and difficult targets Weakly immunogenic antigens, conserved targets, membrane proteins, and conformation-dependent epitopes make it difficult to elicit a sufficiently diverse and functionally potent antibody response.
Affinity and breadth High affinity against a single antigen or a single epitope does not equal a functional advantage; coverage of different subtypes, mutants, or related targets may be insufficient.
Immunogenicity remains “Fully human” or “humanized” does not equal zero immunogenicity; unique variable regions, non-natural sequences, aggregation, and nonspecific binding can all create risk.
Affinity is not developability High-affinity antibodies may still present development risks such as low expression, aggregation, high viscosity, poor stability, polyspecificity, or abnormal clearance.

2. Limitations of current transgenic humanized mice

This section concerns transgenic humanized mice other than human antibody transgenic in vivo models, including human target or gene knock-in mice. Related limitations in using these models for antibody-drug evaluation are included here.

Functional issue Actual limitations of existing platforms
Single-gene humanization does not recreate human pathway or cell context Humanizing a single target or gene does not recreate the human signaling pathway or cellular context in which that target functions.
Partial humanization Many knock-in models humanize only the extracellular domain, or a fragment of the gene, so ligand binding may appear human while transmembrane and intracellular signaling remain murine.
Expression level and pattern differ from human The expression level, tissue distribution, and regulation of a human or humanized gene often differ from those in humans, because the mouse genomic context, promoters, and regulatory elements are not equivalent.
Immune mismatch with the mouse immune system A human or humanized protein still operates in a mouse immune system. Species differences in ligands, receptors, Fc receptors, cytokines, and MHC can distort binding, effector function, and downstream responses. These models cannot fully reproduce human immune-cell interactions, immune tolerance, and inflammatory responses, which limits translational evaluation of immunotherapeutic drugs.
Bias in effector and exposure ranking Human and mouse FcRn/FcγR differ in function and cellular expression, which can bias the ranking of the same antibody for half-life, immune effector function, efficacy, and safety.
Inadequate prediction of cytotoxicity Species differences in target expression, immune-cell composition, and activation thresholds mean that cytotoxicity, immune overactivation, or tissue injury that does not appear in animals may appear in humans.
Nonclinical ADA interference Human-derived antibodies can be xenogeneic proteins in mice and other animals; anti-drug antibodies (ADA) raised in the animal can accelerate clearance and reduce exposure, causing false-negative efficacy or additional toxicity.
Limited disease relevance Transgenic and genetically engineered disease models often fail to capture human pathophysiology, tissue context, or disease heterogeneity, which limits their relevance to clinical settings.

3. Limitations of current human immune system mice

Functional issue Actual limitations of existing platforms
Lack of lymph nodes Current HIS mice lack proper lymph nodes, which limits modeling of the tumor immune microenvironment, induced autoimmune disease, vaccine evaluation, and tumor metastasis, and reduces clinical relevance.
Too few myeloid cells, and high overall mortality Myeloid-cell reconstitution is too low, and overall mortality is high, including GvHD-related decline.
Inter-individual clinical responses are not adequately reflected These models cannot adequately reflect the different responses of different individuals to a drug in the clinic.
No platform to identify clinically relevant immune markers There is a lack of a platform to identify immune-related markers of clinical safety and efficacy, both in vivo and in vitro. Inadequate function of preclinical models can postpone human-specific toxicity, insufficient efficacy, or immune risk until first-in-human studies, with some issues becoming fully apparent only in Phase II.
Insensitive ADA detection Anti-drug antibody (ADA) detection is insensitive.
