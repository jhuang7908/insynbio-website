Intelligent Antibody-Drug Conjugate (ADC) Design

AI-driven end-to-end engineering for next-generation ADCs. From target profiling to linker-payload matching, conjugation technology selection, and CMC validation.

The Therapeutic Window Challenge: ADCs are complex multi-component systems where clinical success is defined by the delicate balance of target specificity, linker stability, and payload potency. A single mismatch—such as pairing a highly hydrophobic payload with a high DAR conjugation method—can lead to aggregation, premature clearance, and severe off-target toxicity.

Our proprietary ADC Decision Engine leverages a comprehensive, QA-audited knowledge base of 199+ validated components and 30+ clinical programs to design best-in-class and first-in-class ADCs. We navigate the vast combinatorial space to recommend designs that maximize efficacy while strictly adhering to safety and CMC constraints.

Design Strategies

Our engine supports multiple design paradigms depending on your clinical goals and risk tolerance:

  • Fast-Follower / Best-in-Class: Targeting validated antigens (e.g., HER2, TROP-2) with optimized linker-payloads (like DXd or TMALIN) to improve the therapeutic window over existing approved ADCs.
  • First-in-Class (Novel Modalities): Designing for emerging targets (e.g., CLDN6, GPC3) or utilizing novel payloads such as PROTACs (to overcome small molecule resistance) or ISACs (Immune-stimulating ADCs for cold tumors).
  • Bispecific ADCs: Dual-targeting (e.g., EGFR x HER3) to enhance tumor specificity, improve internalization, and overcome bypass resistance mechanisms.
  • Masked ADCs (Probody): Utilizing peptide masks cleaved only in the tumor microenvironment to safely target antigens widely expressed on normal tissues.

In Silico CMC & Manufacturability Solutions

ADC manufacturing is notoriously complex due to the risk of aggregation, premature payload shedding, and high Drug-to-Antibody Ratio (DAR) heterogeneity. Our In Silico CMC Engine predicts and mitigates these risks before physical synthesis:

  • Hydrophobicity & Aggregation Prediction: Calculates the overall LogP of the Linker-Payload construct. If a highly hydrophobic payload (e.g., PBD) is paired with a high DAR target, the engine automatically recommends hydrophilic linkers (e.g., PEG spacers or GGFG) or restricts the maximum DAR to prevent High Molecular Weight (HMW) aggregation.
  • DAR Homogeneity Optimization: Evaluates the trade-offs between conjugation technologies. For ultra-potent payloads requiring strict DAR 2.0, we recommend site-specific methods (e.g., THIOMAB, SMARTag, or GlycoConnect) over stochastic cysteine/lysine coupling to minimize un-conjugated antibody and overly toxic high-DAR species.
  • Plasma Stability Modeling: Assesses linker vulnerability to extracellular proteases or spontaneous deconjugation (e.g., maleimide exchange with serum albumin), recommending rigidified linkers or alternative chemistries to ensure the payload reaches the tumor microenvironment.

Case Study: Intelligent Design for Gastric Cancer

Gastric cancer presents a unique challenge due to extreme inter- and intra-tumoral heterogeneity. Here is how the InSynBio ADC Decision Engine navigates the design space compared to historical clinical approaches.

Input Query: disease_type="solid_tumor", disease_subtype="gastric", target="CLDN18.2", proliferation="high"
Layer 1: Target Antigen Profiling (CLDN18.2)
Internalization: Moderate/Slow | Shedding: Very Low | Heterogeneity: High
Decision: Mandatory Bystander Effect (AX-01)
Rationale: CLDN18.2 is a tight-junction protein with sequestered epitopes, leading to moderate internalization. Gastric adenocarcinoma shows high intra-tumoral heterogeneity. To ensure efficacy in CLDN18.2-low regions, the payload must be membrane-permeable.
Layer 2: Payload Selection (TOP1i vs. MMAE)
Tumor Proliferation Index: High (Ki-67 > 50%)
Selected: TOP1i (e.g., DXd, SHR169106)
Rationale: TOP1 inhibitors (DNA damaging) are highly S-phase specific and more effective in rapidly proliferating gastric tumors than tubulin inhibitors. They also provide a superior bystander effect profile for this indication.
Layer 3: Linker & DAR Optimization
Stability vs. Release Efficiency
Selected: GGFG (Tetrapeptide) | DAR: 8.0
Rationale: High DAR (8.0) is recommended to maximize payload delivery per internalization event. A highly hydrophilic GGFG or K-Link is required to maintain CMC stability and prevent aggregation at this high drug load.
Layer 4: Competitive Benchmarking
Comparison with IBI343, CMG901, SHR-A1904
Recommendation: Site-Specific High-DAR TOP1i
Differentiation: While IBI343 uses a similar high-DAR TOP1i strategy, we recommend site-specific conjugation (e.g., engineered Cys) to improve homogeneity and widen the therapeutic window compared to the stochastic mixtures used in earlier generation assets.
