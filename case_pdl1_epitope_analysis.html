Executive Summary

Same Target, Two Binding Configurations, Two Development Pathways

Two anti-PD-L1 antibody clones provided by the client exhibit different in vitro blocking activity — one shows minimal PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition; the other demonstrates strong competitive inhibition. Structural modeling reveals the molecular mechanism and validates cross-species applicability.

Challenge

The client has two binding-validated anti-PD-L1 antibody clones with markedly different PD-1 blocking activities. The molecular mechanism is unknown. The goal: explain the functional difference at the structural level when sequence alone cannot, and provide scientific rationale for two distinct development pathways.

Approach

Used AF2-Multimer to independently model complexes of both antibodies with human PD-L1 and cynomolgus PD-L1. Calculated detailed interface parameters (BSA, shape complementarity, H-bonds, salt bridges), mapped epitope residues, and analyzed overlap with PD-1 competitive binding sites and spatial configurations.

Key Findings

Ab1 binds laterally to the PD-L1 IgC domain extension, not directly occluding the PD-1 binding face — suitable for ADC development with strong tumor targeting and reduced immune-related AEs. Ab2 engages the IgV core frontally, directly competing with PD-1 — ideal for immune checkpoint inhibitor development. Human/cynomolgus binding sites are highly conserved, supporting NHP studies.

Project Background

PD-L1's Dual Therapeutic Role

PD-L1 is a central mediator of tumor immune evasion, highly expressed on tumor cell surfaces, and a key target for both ADC and immune checkpoint inhibitor development. The two development pathways place fundamentally different requirements on antibody binding site location.

🎯 ADC Target Pathway

Non-blocking antibodies bind outside the PD-1 competitive region, without disrupting immune regulation, delivering cytotoxic payloads precisely to PD-L1-overexpressing tumor cells. Compared to blocking ADCs, the risk of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) is lower with a wider safety window.

🛡 Immune Checkpoint Pathway

Blocking antibodies directly occupy the PD-1 binding interface on PD-L1, lifting the tumor cell suppression of T cell activation and reactivating the immune system's tumor killing. Atezolizumab and Durvalumab are approved antibodies of this pathway.

🔬 Structure Defines Function

The key differentiator between the two pathways is epitope location and binding configuration — not affinity level. AF2 structural modeling provides structure-level evidence to classify clones before experimental validation, substantially shortening the development decision cycle.

Epitope Classification

Epitope Overlap and Differential Region Mapping

Both antibodies share 14 residues in the PD-L1 IgV core region (covering key PD-1 binding positions), but their unique contact regions determine fundamentally different functions. Ab1 extends further to the lateral IgC domain face; Ab2 goes deep into the N-terminal IgV groove.

Shared (14): 38TYR, 40GLU, 42GLU, 43ASP, 45ASN, 48GLN, 58VAL, 95ARG, 97MET, 103ALA, 104ASP, 105TYR, 106LYS, 107ARG
Ab1 Unique (12): IgC extension — 91ALA, 92GLY, 93VAL, 94TYR, 108–111, 171LYS, etc.
Ab2 Unique (9): IgV N-terminal groove — 1PHE, 36ILE, 50VAL, 51HIS, 53GLU, 55ASP, etc.
Interface Parameter Comparison
Epitope Distribution

Ab1 — Lateral Binding (Non-blocking)

  • Epitope extends to IgC domain lateral face (91–111, 171), far from PD-1 binding face
  • Extensive H-bonds (32 pairs) maintain broad but relatively flat contact network
  • Binding angle from the lateral face of PD-L1; does not occlude PD-1 binding groove
  • Low spatial competition with PD-1 → non-blocking or weakly blocking profile
  • Recommended pathway: ADC (precise targeting, fewer side effects)

Ab2 — Frontal Engagement (Blocking)

  • Epitope concentrated in IgV core, including N-terminal groove (1PHE, 36ILE)
  • 5 salt bridges + 11 hydrophobic contacts provide strong directional binding force
  • Binding angle frontally covers PD-1 binding interface (CC′ and FG loops)
  • Directly competes with key PD-1 residues (R113, M115, D122 corresponding sites)
  • Recommended pathway: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor
Cross-Species Validation

Human / Cynomolgus PD-L1 Binding Site Conservation

Preclinical drug development typically requires non-human primate (NHP) toxicology data. AF2 modeling validated binding of both antibodies to both human and cynomolgus PD-L1, providing structural justification for NHP study design.

🧬 Human PD-L1 (Homo sapiens)

AF2 modeled complex structures show high-confidence binding interfaces for both Ab1 and Ab2 (ipTM > 0.70). Core epitope residues 38TYR, 40GLU, 95ARG, 97MET, and segment 104–107 are fully covered with excellent shape complementarity.

🐒 Cynomolgus PD-L1 (Macaca fascicularis)

Cynomolgus PD-L1 shares high sequence homology with human PD-L1 in the key epitope segment (IgV domain 38–107). AF2 modeling shows both antibodies adopt highly similar binding poses to cynomolgus vs. human PD-L1, with conserved binding sites — supporting NHP pharmacology, toxicology, and safety study designs.

Cross-Species Conclusion: Binding epitopes of both antibodies are highly conserved between human and cynomolgus PD-L1. Structural predictions support pharmacodynamic, toxicological, and safety assessment in cynomolgus macaque models, meeting the species relevance requirements for IND filing.
Development Strategy

Structure-Defined Dual-Pathway Development

Based on structural analysis, the two antibodies each enter distinct development pathways that maximize their respective clinical value.

Pathway A · Non-blocking

Ab1 → PD-L1 ADC

Lateral epitope · No PD-1 competition · Low irAE risk

  • Binds PD-L1 IgC lateral region; does not block PD-1/PD-L1 signaling pathway
  • Precisely targets PD-L1-overexpressing tumor cells for cytotoxic payload internalization
  • Avoids systemic T cell activation; fewer immune-related adverse events (irAEs) expected
  • High potential for combination with existing checkpoint inhibitors (complementary mechanism)
  • Suitable for NSCLC, TNBC, and other PD-L1-high indications
Pathway B · Blocking

Ab2 → Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor

IgV core epitope · Direct PD-1 competition · Classic immune activation

  • Frontally covers the PD-1 binding interface; competitively blocks PD-1/PD-L1 interaction
  • Lifts tumor inhibition of T cell activation; reactivates anti-tumor immunity
  • Mechanism similar to Atezolizumab (Tecentriq) and Durvalumab (Imfinzi)
  • IgV core binding ensures high epitope conservation across clonal variants
  • Can be developed as monoclonal or bispecific antibody (combined with CTLA-4, LAG3, etc.)
Methods & Limitations

Prediction Reliability and Experimental Validation

AF2 structural analysis provides high-quality directional guidance, but experimental validation is required before all critical decisions.

AF2 Modeling Reliability

  • Ab2 complex ipTM = 0.720, pTM = 0.770 — high-confidence interface prediction
  • Both antibody VH/VL pLDDT > 91 — very high structural prediction confidence
  • BSA and shape complementarity (SC) robustly describe overall interface character
  • 5 Å contact threshold is permissive; peripheral residues (e.g., IgC tail) may include false positives
  • Interpret IgV domain (1–127) epitopes as primary; IgC extension as supplementary

Recommended Experimental Validation

  • Competition ELISA: PD-1-Fc vs. both antibodies for PD-L1 binding; quantify % blocking
  • BLI / SPR: determine binding kinetics (kon, koff, KD) and PD-1 competitive potency
  • Epitope binning: confirm whether the two antibodies have independent (non-competing) epitopes
  • Cross-species: separately assess binding affinity to human vs. cynomolgus PD-L1-ECD
  • Functional assay: MLR to confirm T cell activation for the blocking clone
